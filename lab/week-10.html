Lab guide 10

Vulnerability Management and Monitoring

Finding problems systematically, and building the visibility to catch the next one before it's an incident.

10-12 hours Week 9 complete
Before you start
  • secure-target and kali-tools VMs up

1. Run a real vulnerability scan

From identification straight through to a written finding.

From kali-tools, scan secure-target for service versions
nmap -sV --script vuln secure-target.lab 2>/dev/null | head -40   # nmap's vuln script set does a lightweight check

2. Prioritize and remediate

Not every finding gets fixed today. Severity assessment (often CVSS-driven) combines with business context to prioritize. A penetration test report adds real-world exploitability on top of a scanner's theoretical findings — a scanner might flag ten "critical" issues, but a pen tester confirming three are actually exploitable from the internet tells you where to start. After remediation, verification — rescanning to confirm the fix actually worked — is not optional.

3. Aggregate and tail logs from multiple sources

The raw material every monitoring tool is built on.

Central log view across services
sudo journalctl --since '1 hour ago' -p err --no-pager | tail -20
sudo journalctl -u sshd --no-pager | tail -10

4. What a SIEM adds that a log file doesn't

A plain log file is passive — someone has to go read it. A Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) system ingests logs from every source, correlates events across them in real time, and fires automated alerts when a pattern matches — like the same account failing login on three different systems within a minute. SOAR (orchestration) goes one step further and can automatically respond, not just alert.

5. Monitoring protocols, briefly

NetFlow records connection metadata (who talked to whom, how much data) without capturing full packet content — lighter weight than a full packet capture. SNMP monitors network device health. syslog is the standard format/transport most Linux logging speaks. SCAP (Security Content Automation Protocol) standardizes how vulnerability and configuration checks are expressed so tools from different vendors can share results.

6. Write a one-page remediation report

The deliverable this whole week has been building toward.

A minimal template — fill it in from your own Week 10 scan
cat > ~/secplus-lab/week10-report.txt <<'EOF'
Finding: [service/version from your nmap scan]
Severity: [your assessment, with reasoning]
Remediation: [patch/config change needed]
Verification: [how you'd confirm the fix — rescan command]
EOF
cat ~/secplus-lab/week10-report.txt

Check your work

Run these before you consider the lab finished.

  1. The nmap vuln scan completed and you can name at least one real finding
  2. journalctl output shows aggregated logs across at least two services
  3. week10-report.txt exists and has all four fields filled in
  4. You can explain what NetFlow captures that a full packet capture doesn't, and why that trade-off is sometimes preferred
