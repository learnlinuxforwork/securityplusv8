Lab guide 4

Threat Vectors and Attack Surfaces

Every way in — message, image, browser, network, supply chain, physical, and signal-based.

8-10 hours Week 3 complete
Before you start
  • secure-target and kali-tools VMs up

1. Message-, image-, and attachment-based vectors

Message-based covers email, SMS, RCS, instant messaging, and collaboration tools — the classic phishing surface, now spread across a lot more than just email. Image-based vectors are sneakier: a malicious QR code, embedded content inside an image, or an abused CAPTCHA flow. Attachment-based vectors hide payloads in embedded macros, RTF documents, and even PDFs — file formats people trust by default.

2. Browser-, network-, and remote-access-based vectors

Browser-based: malicious extensions, injected JavaScript, stolen cookies and session tokens. Network-based: compromised infrastructure devices, virtualized devices, stolen session keys. Remote access: abused RDP, VNC, or VPN sessions — legitimate remote-access tools turned against you are one of the most common real-world footholds.

3. Firewall a vector shut, and see what it does and doesn't stop

One rule, one clear boundary.

Block inbound traffic to a port on secure-target, then confirm from kali-tools
# On secure-target:
sudo firewall-cmd --add-port=8080/tcp --permanent 2>/dev/null; sudo firewall-cmd --reload 2>/dev/null || sudo ufw deny 8080/tcp
sudo firewall-cmd --list-ports 2>/dev/null || sudo ufw status
From kali-tools, confirm the port is no longer reachable, but SSH still is
nmap -p 22,8080 secure-target.lab

4. Endpoint-, supply chain-, and external media-based vectors

Endpoint-based covers every device type (mobile, workstation, server, tablet) and a sneaky category: living-off-the-land tools — an attacker using tools already trusted and present on the system (bash, curl, cron, built-in scripting utilities) instead of dropping obvious malware. Supply chain-based means the compromise comes through a third-party provider, MSP, logistics provider, or SaaS vendor you trust — the SolarWinds and similar incidents live here. External media-based is the classic malicious USB drop.

5. Human-, IoT-, OT-, physical-, and signal-based vectors

Human-based: impersonation (posing as a contractor or visitor), a watering-hole attack (compromising a site your target already visits). IoT-based: cameras, sensors, printers — often shipped with default credentials and never patched. OT-based (operational technology) covers industrial control systems. Physical-based: lock and key, access vestibules, access passes. Signal-based: Bluetooth, RF, and NFC — short-range but real, especially against badge and payment systems.

Check your work

Run these before you consider the lab finished.

  1. nmap -p 8080 secure-target.lab shows the port closed/filtered
  2. nmap -p 22 secure-target.lab shows ssh still reachable
  3. You can name all 12 threat vector categories from memory and give one real example of each
