Lab guide 15

Mock Exam, Remediation, and Exam Day

A full timed mock exam under real conditions, remediation of everything you missed, and exam-day logistics.

10-12 hours Weeks 1-14 complete
Before you start
  • A quiet, uninterrupted block of time for the mock exam
  • CompTIA's own acronym list, printed or open in a second tab for final review

1. Sit a full timed practice exam

Set aside a distraction-free block matching CompTIA's real exam conditions as closely as you can — no notes, no lookups mid-question, timed. Community-written practice question sets and CompTIA's own official practice materials are both reasonable sources; be selective about "brain dump" sites, which CompTIA explicitly prohibits using and can revoke a certification over.

2. Score yourself by domain, honestly

Tally your results against all five domains:

  • Domain 1 — General Security Concepts (16%): ___ correct
  • Domain 2 — Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Attacks (24%): ___ correct
  • Domain 3 — Security Architecture (19%): ___ correct
  • Domain 4 — Security Operations (27%): ___ correct
  • Domain 5 — Security Program Management and Oversight (14%): ___ correct

Your weakest domain, weighted by how much of the exam it represents, is this week's actual homework — not just your lowest raw score.

3. Redo the weakest domain's labs, cold

Untimed, guide closed, from memory.

Match your weakest domain to its weeks and redo them
# Domain 1 -> Weeks 1-2   Domain 2 -> Weeks 3-6
# Domain 3 -> Weeks 7-8    Domain 4 -> Weeks 9-12
# Domain 5 -> Weeks 13-14
# Redo every hands-on step in that range without looking, then check yourself against the guide.

4. Drill the acronym list until it's automatic

CompTIA publishes a full acronym list alongside the objectives — HIDS vs. NIDS vs. WIPS, RTO vs. RPO, SIEM vs. SOAR, MOU vs. MOA vs. NDA vs. MSA. This exam rewards knowing these cold, not "recognizing them eventually." If you have to think for more than a second, it's not learned yet — drill it again.

5. Confirm your exam-day logistics

Government-issued photo ID matching your CompTIA account exactly. Confirm your testing method — Pearson VUE test center or online proctored — and if online, run the system compatibility check the day before, not the morning of. Clear your desk completely for an online exam: no notes, no books, no second monitor, no phone in the room.

Check your work

Run these before you consider the lab finished.

  1. A completed, honestly-scored practice exam with domain-by-domain results recorded
  2. Your weakest domain's labs redone from memory, checked against the original guides
  3. You can define every acronym on CompTIA's published list without hesitating
  4. A booked exam date, or a clear plan for when you'll book it
